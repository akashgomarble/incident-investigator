# Incident investigation report: INC-002
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
The portfolio page timeouts and api-gateway 504s were caused by config change cfg-2207, which shrank portfolio-service's HikariCP datasource pool from 50 to 5 connections at 2026-03-10T09:52:00Z; the shrunken pool exhausted immediately, queuing 25-57 concurrent /v1/portfolio/holdings waiters that timed out after 3000ms, surfacing as portfolio-service 503s that the gateway logged as upstream timeouts and returned as 504s. Confidence is high (0.85): the pool-capacity collapse (flat ~10.8-12.7 connections through the 09:52Z bucket, then pinned at exactly 5.0 from 09:53Z) and the portfolio-service p99 jump (172.69ms -> 4250.91ms at the same bucket boundary) both occur at the first sample after the cfg-2207 timestamp, and precede the first acquisition error log (09:53:19Z) and first gateway 504 (09:53:53Z). The reporter's HTTP/2 suspicion (dep-5220) is contradicted: it predates onset by ~1h48m, portfolio-service was perfectly healthy for that entire interval, a gateway stall cannot produce total=5/active=5/idle=0 with 25-57 waiters, and its rollout scope (portfolio AND order upstreams) exceeds the observed blast radius (/v1/portfolio/* only). The shared-ledger-db alternative was actively tested rather than merely dismissed — order-, payment- and auth-service all show flat latency/error and stable, unsaturated pools across onset. Fix: revert cfg-2207 to restore the portfolio-service pool size.

## Probable root cause
**cfg-2207 cost-optimisation profile shrank portfolio-service DB pool 50→5, exhausting connections** -- config_change in `portfolio-service`, triggered by `cfg-2207` (confidence 0.72, medium)

### H1: cfg-2207 cost-optimisation profile shrank portfolio-service DB pool 50→5, exhausting connections
At 09:52:00Z infra-automation applied cfg-2207 ('Apply cost-optimisation profile "small" to service datasource'), reducing portfolio-service's HikariCP db.pool.max_size from 50 to 5. The minute-level series resolves the onset ordering: portfolio-service db_pool_in_use was flat at ~10.8-12.7 connections through the 09:52:00Z bucket (11.89), then fell to exactly 5.0 at 09:53:00Z and stayed pinned at 5.0 thereafter — i.e. usable capacity collapsed at the first sample after the config timestamp, not a gradual climb. portfolio-service p99 jumped at the identical 09:52→09:53Z transition (172.69ms → 4250.91ms). With only 5 connections, concurrent /v1/portfolio/holdings requests queued (25-57 waiters) and timed out after 3000ms; the JDBC acquisition failures surfaced as portfolio-service 503s, which the gateway logged as upstream timeouts and returned as 504s on /v1/portfolio/*, driving gateway p99 to ~4.3s. Fault originates in portfolio-service's datasource configuration, not the gateway.

**Causal chain**
1. cfg-2207 applied at 09:52:00Z cuts portfolio-service db.pool.max_size 50 -> 5; it is the only portfolio-service config change since 2026-03-09 [F2, F3, F16, F17]
2. Effective pool capacity collapses at the first sample after 09:52Z: db_pool_in_use ~11.89 at 09:52Z -> exactly 5.0 at 09:53Z, pinned at 5.0 thereafter; the pool did NOT climb toward a ceiling before 09:52Z [F31]
3. With 5 connections, concurrent portfolio/holdings requests queue beyond the pool [F12]
4. HikariPool saturates: total=5, active=5, idle=0, 25-57 waiters, all timing out after 3000ms [F12, F11]

### H3: Shared ledger-db degradation/failover saturates all callers' DB connections, hitting the smallest pool (portfolio-service) first
portfolio-service's JDBC exhaustion need not originate in its own pool config: if ledger-db (shared=true, used by portfolio-, order-, auth- and payment-service) became slow or failed over around 09:48-09:52Z, every caller's connections would be held longer, exhausting pools. Portfolio-service's pool is the smallest (5 after cfg-2207), so it crossed into 503s first and the gateway saw 504s on /v1/portfolio/*. This matches HIST-102 ('Portfolio timeouts during ledger-db failover', 504 at gateway). The alternative remains alive because ledger-db exposes zero change/deploy telemetry and no operational-event signal class is available in this toolset; it is not dismissed by the observed data, only by absence of expected neighbour symptoms.

**Causal chain**
1. ledger-db is shared=true and backs portfolio-service, order-service, auth-service and payment-service [F5, F19]
2. ledger-db slowed or failed over near 09:48-09:52Z (unobserved — no change/deploy/telemetry record for ledger-db exists in the toolset) *(assumed)*
3. Slower queries hold connections longer, saturating pools in dependent services *(assumed)*
4. portfolio-service pool saturates (total=5, active=5, waiters 25-57) and returns JDBC acquisition timeouts [F11, F12]
5. Gateway surfaces upstream timeouts and 504s on /v1/portfolio/* [F8, F9]

**Supporting evidence**

### H2: dep-5220 gateway HTTP/2 upgrade to the portfolio-service upstream (envoy 1.30) causes the latency and upstream timeouts

Under h2 the gateway multiplexes many requests over few connections; if envoy 1.30's h2 handling resets or stalls streams, upstream request timeouts on /v1/portfolio/* would surface directly as gateway p99 ~4.3s and 504s, with no DB saturation needed. This is the reporter's suspicion and matches HIST-116 ('HTTP/2 resets after gateway envoy upgrade', fixed by rolling back the gateway).

### H4: Redis-cache (or market-data-service/exchange-gateway) dependency slowness stalls portfolio-service request processing
The portfolio path includes portfolio-service -> redis-cache and portfolio-service -> market-data-service -> {redis-cache, exchange-gateway}. Degradation of the shared redis-cache or of market-data-service/exchange-gateway could block portfolio-service request threads, inflating in-flight DB demand until the pool saturated. The origin would be downstream of portfolio-service, not its pool config and not the gateway.

**Causal chain**
1. portfolio-service depends on market-data-service (http) and redis-cache (cache); market-data-service depends on redis-cache and exchange-gateway [F4, F5]
2. One of these dependencies degraded near 09:48-09:52Z, slowing portfolio-service request handling *(assumed)*
3. Slow handling holds DB connections longer until the pool saturates *(assumed)*
4. portfolio-service JDBC acquisition timeouts -> gateway upstream timeouts -> 504 on /v1/portfolio/* [F11, F12, F8, F9]

## What would change this conclusion
- Evidence that portfolio-service db_pool_in_use was already climbing or saturating before 09:52Z (before cfg-2207) — this would defeat the causal hop that the 50->5 reduction caused the exhaustion rather than a pre-existing contention being resolved into saturation by an unrelated factor. The 1-min series currently shows a flat ~10.8-12.7 band through 09:52Z, so this would require contradicting that series.
- Recovery that does not follow the cfg-2207 revert: if pool saturation and 504s persist after restoring db.pool.max_size to 50, the origin lies elsewhere (e.g. ledger-db/connection path), demoting H1.
- Direct evidence of a ledger-db failover, restart, maintenance window or parameter change at 09:40-10:00Z (an operational-event record or DB-side latency/connection anomaly), or observed DB latency/error degradation in order-service/payment-service/auth-service — any of these would revive H3.
- Evidence that the clean neighbour metrics were an artefact (e.g. order-/payment-service connected to a different ledger-db replica than portfolio-service, or per-upstream gateway route splits showing broad degradation beyond /v1/portfolio/*), which would weaken the scope argument against H3 and H2.
- Rolling back dep-5220 (envoy 1.30 / HTTP/2) and observing that the timeouts stop while cfg-2207 remains in place would support H2; conversely, reproduction of envoy-1.30/HTTP-2 stream stalls under load on the portfolio upstream without any pool limitation would reopen the deployment hypothesis.
- A portfolio-service request_rate series showing a local inbound surge at ~09:52Z independent of gateway traffic would introduce a load-driven explanation competing with the pool-size change.
