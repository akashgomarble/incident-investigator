# Incident investigation report: INC-004
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
notification-service's repeated OOMKilled restarts (exit 137) are a self-consistent ~23h sawtooth memory leak whose onset is co-located with dep-5402 (v3.8.0, 2026-03-21T00:00Z), which added an in-process TemplateCache (ConcurrentHashMap) on the render path with no bound/TTL/eviction visible anywhere in the deploy or config record. Memory ramps gradually and monotonically from ~48% to ~98% over ~22h and resets on restart, verified across all three cycles (3/21, 3/22, 3/23), and the pre-deploy baseline (3/20) is flat and reset-free with zero restarts before 3/21T22:59:50Z. The reported "Kafka replay after maintenance" narrative is contradicted: memory was already ~88% at 16:00Z and rising well before the 18:00Z cfg-2410 retention change, the lag spikes FOLLOW the OOM rather than precede it, the co-consumer settlement-service stayed flat, and identical cycles ran on 3/21 and 3/22 before any 3/23 change. Confidence is medium (~0.70): the top hypothesis is clearly ahead and no competing hypothesis names a different origin, but the load-bearing claim that the cache is effectively unbounded is inference from absence of parameters (the dep-5402 diff/implementation is not retrievable) and the pre-deploy baseline covers only ~1 day.

## Probable root cause
**In-memory TemplateCache introduced by dep-5402 grows without bound/without effective eviction until OOM** -- bad_deployment in `notification-service`, triggered by `dep-5402` (confidence 0.70, medium)

### H1: In-memory TemplateCache introduced by dep-5402 grows without bound/without effective eviction until OOM
dep-5402 (notification-service v3.8.0, 2026-03-21T00:00Z) added an in-process ConcurrentHashMap TemplateCache on the render path. Because no size bound/TTL/eviction policy is configured for it (and no config or deploy change anywhere in the window sets or removes one), heap occupancy climbs monotonically over roughly one day from ~48% to ~98%, old-gen saturates with long full GCs, and the container is OOMKilled (exit 137). The restart resets memory to ~48% and the cycle repeats at ~23h, delaying trade-confirmation emails. Cache growth is the dominant leak mechanism; the exact fraction of the ramp attributable to the cache versus other allocation paths is unresolved, and 'no bound configured' is absence-of-mention rather than proof of absence.

**Causal chain**
1. dep-5402 adds an in-memory TemplateCache (ConcurrentHashMap keyed template+locale+userSegment) on the render path; the record states no size bound, TTL or eviction and shows status live / rolled_back_at null [F2, F20, F32]
2. No configuration anywhere in this service or the estate bounds a cache: notification-service has zero config changes, and the only estate-wide change is Kafka retention [F22, F23, F21, F33]
3. The cache accumulates entries with zero evictions and grows to 188100 entries (single sample), and the app logs sustained old-gen heap-over-90 WARNs with full GCs up to 6488ms [F16, F38, F40]
4. Heap grows monotonically roughly 48%->98% per ~22h cycle, with max 98.44%; growth onset is co-located with the dep-5402 activation boundary versus a flat ~47-48% pre-deploy period [F9, F15, F27]
5. The container hits its memory limit and is OOMKilled with exit code 137 (same pod notification-service-7d9f), then restarts [F7, F13]
6. After the restart, memory resets to ~48% and lag bursts in the bucket straddling the OOM then drains back to normal — lag is an effect of the restart, not the cause [F9, F10, F17]
7. The ~23h ramp-and-OOM cycle repeats across 3/21, 3/22 and 3/23 with the same pre-OOM old-gen signature, producing delayed trade-confirmation emails [F7, F8, F19]

### H3: Kafka maintenance is a red herring: lag spikes are the effect of notification-service restarting (corollary of H1, not an independent origin)
The consumer lag spikes reported as the symptom do not cause the OOM; they are caused by it. Each OOMKill freezes the consumer, lag accumulates in the bucket straddling the kill, and the restarted pod drains the backlog back to normal. The 3/23 Kafka retention change is coincidental and had no causal role — memory was already ~88% before it and rising. This is best read as a supporting observation about ordering (lag follows OOM, config change coincidental) rather than a competing root origin, since it names no independent cause; it is retained as the surviving non-broker reading of the lag symptom.

**Causal chain**
1. Memory ramps monotonically to the limit and the pod is OOMKilled [F9, F7]
2. Before the 3/23 change memory was already ~88% at 16:00Z and rose through 18:00Z without inflection — the maintenance did not create or amplify growth [F14, F13]
3. 20-min zoom shows lag normal (~60-77) until the restart, then exploding in the bucket straddling the OOM [F10, F17]
4. Lag bursts recur tied to the restart cycle (same ~20:00/22:00 hours across days), not to load or maintenance events [F11]
5. The co-consumer settlement-service on the same shared broker stays flat and low across the same window, so no broker-wide replay drove the burst [F18]

**Supporting evidence**
  - `F10` ✔ A ~2h-bucket view makes consumer_lag look like it spikes in the same bucket as each restart (e.g. 2026-03-21T22:00 avg 2273.11, max 17161.92), but a 20-min zoom shows the spike follows the OOM: lag is normal (~60-77) through 22:20 and only explodes in the 22:40 bucket (avg 4334.88, max 17161.92) after the 22:59 restart.
  - `F17` ✔ Consumer lag does NOT lead the 3/23 OOM: lag is normal (~49-71) through 19:30Z and only bursts in the 19:45Z bucket (avg 6082.94, max 18137.93) and 20:00Z bucket (avg 10696.07) i.e. straddling the 19:59:21Z restart, returning to ~64/50 by 20:30-20:45Z. Lag is an effect of the restart, not the cause of the memory ramp.
  - `F11` ✔ Notable lag spikes recur in the same ~22:00/20:00 hours across days: 2026-03-21T22:00 max 17161.92, 2026-03-22T20:00 avg 790.21 max 17611.66 / 22:00 avg 1589.77, 2026-03-23T18:00 avg 810.33 max 18137.93 / 20:00 avg 1542.68 — i.e. the lag bursts are tied to the restart cycle, not to a baseline load shift.
  - `F18` ✔ The lag spike is NOT a shared-broker/replay symptom: settlement-service (the other consumer on the shared kafka broker) shows flat, low lag across the exact same 18:00-23:00Z window (avg 32-56, max 82.06), with no spike at 19:45-20:00Z when notification-service hit 18137.93. This argues the 3/23 lag burst is local to notification-service's own restart, not a broker-wide replay; Kafka maintenance was not needed to produce it.
  - `F14` ✔ Zoomed to 20-min buckets on 2026-03-23, memory was already ~88% at 16:00Z and rose monotonically to 95.96% at 19:00Z and 96.73%/98.44% at 19:40Z, then reset to 46.72% in the 20:00 bucket. The ramp therefore began well before the 18:00Z Kafka retention change and continued through it — the config change did not create or inflect the growth.
  - `F13` ✔ All three OOMKilled events are the same pod/same signature (exit code 137, container notification-service-7d9f), and the 3/23 event fired at 2026-03-23T19:59:21Z — 1h59m AFTER the 18:00Z Kafka maintenance — while the pod was already at ~96% memory. So the OOM was not triggered at maintenance time; memory was already near limit 2h before.
**Contradicting evidence**
  - (none)
**Assumptions**
  - The lag-vs-OOM ordering established at 20-min resolution for the 3/21 restart holds for 3/23 as well (the 3/23 fine-grained lag query failed with 503; the 3/23 ordering rests on 2h buckets)
  - Lag bursts fully drain without manual intervention, consistent with restart-triggered backlog

### H5: Memory growth originates in the consumer/render path rather than the cache (cache bounded or incidental); leak origin unattributed
dep-5402 changed the render path and its memory profile, but the growth driver is NOT (or not predominantly) the TemplateCache: either the cache key-space is effectively bounded (templates x locales x userSegments) so 188100 is a high-water mark with no evictions needed, or the dominant allocation is on the consumer/message-processing path that dep-5402 altered. In that reading dep-5402 remains the most likely trigger (growth onset is co-located with its activation) but the specific mechanism inside the release is unattributed, and the fix is not 'make the cache bounded' but re-instrumenting and reverting/patching the release. This hypothesis exists to carry the case the critic flags: if the cache is bounded, H1's stated mechanism collapses to a temporal association.

**Causal chain**
1. dep-5402 is the only change co-located with growth onset; pre-deploy 3/20 memory is flat at ~48% with zero restarts [F15, F27, F28]
2. The cache's key space may be intrinsically bounded (templates x locales x segments) so a no-eviction high-water mark is expected and 188100 need not imply unbounded growth [F24, F42]
3. Per-cycle cache maximum is obtainable for only one of three cycles, and the high-water sample resets toward ~0 after restart, so cache growth is not proven to track the heap ramp [F42, F43]
4. The ramp could instead be a slow accumulation on the consumer/render path introduced by the same release; no bytes-per-entry or component breakdown exists to separate the two [F41]
5. Either way the pod reaches ~98% and is OOMKilled on a ~23h cycle, then resets and repeats [F9, F7, F19]

**Supporting evidence**
