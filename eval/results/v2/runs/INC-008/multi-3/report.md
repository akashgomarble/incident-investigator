# Incident investigation report: INC-008
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
The best-supported explanation (H2, confidence 0.60, medium) is a demand-side capacity saturation of auth-service: a genuine ~3.8x step in inbound login traffic beginning ~06:50Z overwhelmed auth-service's fixed worker pool (64) and maxReplicas 8, filling the accept queue and producing ~4s p99 latencies and 503s. The competing retry-amplification hypothesis (H5, 0.18) is weakened by ordering evidence at both api-gateway and auth-service: request volume climbs monotonically from ~06:49-06:50Z while error rates and p99 stay at baseline for another ~4-5 minutes, the reverse of the retry-after-failure order H5 requires. The reporter's bcrypt-regression theory (H1) is refuted — no auth-service deployment exists anywhere in April 2026 — and the shared-redis (H3) and dependency-stall (H4) hypotheses are excluded on timing, flat dependency metrics, and the accept-queue-full (not downstream-timeout) 503 signature. Confidence is capped at 0.60 (not higher) because the origin of the demand surge cannot be attributed to a specific caller, region, or tenant: the decisive instrumentation (per-caller log fields, retry/attempt metrics) does not exist, so the "genuine external demand vs. edge/client regeneration" hop remains an assumption rather than a verified fact.

## Probable root cause
**Demand step: ~3.8x surge in login requests saturates auth-service capacity (retry storm / caller burst)** -- capacity_saturation in `auth-service (demand-side origin: external caller / client burst; source unattributed)`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.60, medium)

### H2: Demand step: ~3.8x surge in login requests saturates auth-service capacity (retry storm / caller burst)
A demand-side event began around 06:50Z, stepping requests to POST /v1/auth/login from ~395 to ~1500 req/s (and api-gateway inbound from ~1250 to ~4900 req/s). auth-service's fixed worker pool (64) and maxReplicas 8 cannot absorb it, so the accept queue fills, CPU pins at ~97%, and 503s are returned. Load preceded errors and latency at both the gateway and auth-service, which is what a genuine demand step (rather than retry regeneration) produces. The origin is the demand source itself; whether that source is an external client burst or client/gateway retries cannot be attributed because auth-service and gateway logs carry no caller/region/tenant/cid field.

**Causal chain**
1. A demand-side event (external caller burst) begins around 06:50Z — no attributed source could be identified (log records carry no caller/region/cid: F33) *(assumed)*
2. api-gateway inbound request_rate climbs first and monotonically from ~1212 (06:49) to 2165 (06:52), then ~4871-4955 req/s sustained; it is NOT a pass-through of auth-service's count (~3.2x differential) [F34, F37, F38]

### H5: Client/gateway retry amplification regenerates login calls, self-sustaining the overload after an initial trigger
An initial transient latency blip on the login path caused clients or the api-gateway to retry POST /v1/auth/login; each retry adds load, multiplying effective demand ~3.8x and self-sustaining the overload. The origin is retry/amplification behaviour at the client or gateway layer, not auth-service code. This is now WEAKENED by ordering evidence: load rises BEFORE errors/latency at both gateway (F38/F39) and auth-service, which is the reverse of what retry-after-failure requires. It remains active only because retry amplification cannot be directly measured (no retry metric: F40) and no per-caller attribution exists (F33).

**Causal chain**
1. An initial latency blip on the login path triggers client/gateway retries — assumed; no blip precedes the load step (pre-onset p99 is a flat ~90ms baseline for auth-service, ~200-214ms for gateway) *(assumed)*
2. Retries are counted as fresh inbound requests; api-gateway request_rate rises to ~4900 req/s and auth-service to ~1500 req/s [F34, F10, F31]
3. Gateway load precedes gateway errors/latency (error_rate and p99 flat until 06:54-06:55) — the opposite ordering from what retry regeneration predicts [F38, F39]
4. auth-service worker pool (64) and maxReplicas 8 cannot absorb the multiplied load; accept queue fills; 503 on /v1/auth/login feeds further retries [F11, F12, F32, F36]
