# Incident investigation report: INC-009
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
Root cause: portfolio-service v4.5.0 (dep-5905, deployed 08:56Z) added an unindexed query on the shared ledger-db that ran a sequential scan over the 48.2M-row trades table, saturating the shared database (CPU ~95%, active connections ~190-212, slow queries ~140/min) and starving order-service's INSERT INTO orders writes, which then hit statement timeouts, saturated order-service's connection pool and drove p99 to ~5s. The evidence is tight on origin: dep-5905 is the only fleet-wide deployment; portfolio-service is a verified co-tenant writer of ledger-db; the exact "Seq Scan on trades ... application_name=portfolio-service" signature has zero occurrences before the deploy and 360/360 after, and is the sole slow-query species in ledger-db's logs while order-service contributes none. Both the reporter's autovacuum suspicion (cfg-2903, 55 minutes before onset, with flat ledger-db baselines until 08:56Z) and an independent infrastructure fault are rejected by the record. Confidence in the origin is high, with one calibration caveat: how much of ledger-db's aggregate slow-query counter/connections belongs to the trades query vs other tenants cannot be apportioned from available telemetry, because ledger-db exposes no per-statement or per-application counter. The condition is not self-limiting — the trade scan was still running at full rate at 10:58Z.

## Probable root cause
**Co-tenant portfolio-service deploy introduced an unindexed seq-scan query on shared ledger-db, saturating the DB and stalling order-service writes** -- bad_deployment in `portfolio-service`, triggered by `dep-5905` (confidence 0.60, medium)

### H1: Co-tenant portfolio-service deploy introduced an unindexed seq-scan query on shared ledger-db, saturating the DB and stalling order-service writes
dep-5905 (portfolio-service v4.5.0, 08:56Z, new query on trades filtered by account_id + executed_at, no migration/index) began issuing a sequential scan on the shared ledger-db 'trades' table (48.2M rows). This drove ledger-db CPU to ~95%, active connections from ~61 to ~190-212, and slow queries from ~1/min to ~140/min, starving order-service's INSERT INTO orders writes, which hit statement timeouts, saturating order-service's db_pool and driving p99 to ~5s. The 48.2M-row trades seq scan is portfolio-service's query, NOT an order-service scan (order-service has zero slow-query log presence). Load-bearing hop remaining unmeasured: that the trades query accounts for a DOMINANT share of the ~140/min counter and the ~190 connections — F27 only proves portfolio-service owns 100% of *logged* slow queries (a threshold-filtered sample), not the counter's composition. The counter surplus could in principle be the same query class logged at threshold (logging captures only slow executions, not total executions), which would make 3/min of log lines fully consistent with a much larger true execution rate and require no second query species.

**Causal chain**
1. dep-5905 deploys portfolio-service v4.5.0 at 08:56Z with a new trades query (account_id + executed_at) and no index/migration [F1, F14, F15]
2. portfolio-service is a co-tenant writing to the shared ledger-db, so its query load lands on the same instance order-service uses [F5, F18, F4]

### H3: Latent capacity saturation on ledger-db from aggregate multi-tenant load, with no identified origin (origin-less residue claim)

This hypothesis now rests ONLY on the F28 arithmetic gap (the ~140/min counter is ~46x the ~3/min implied by the 360 logged trade scans) plus the unfalsifiable claim that some unmeasured load drove the residue. Its original autovacuum trigger (cfg-2903) is temporally dead and has been removed from the causal chain. The critique correctly notes that a logging-threshold sampling effect fully explains the counter-vs-log gap without requiring any second query class, and that no pre-08:54Z ramp exists on any ledger-db signal (F37-F40), which removes the latent-build-up reading this hypothesis needed.

## Data gaps
- No per-statement (pg_stat_statements/calls) or per-application_name connection counter exists on ledger-db; available_metrics are only connections_active, cpu_pct, disk_used_pct, replication_lag_ms, slow_queries_per_min, and log searches for 'connection' and for 'application_name=order-service' returned zero matches. This prevents apportioning the ~140/min slow-query plateau, ~190 connections and ~95% CPU between portfolio-service's trades query and other tenants/order-service writes — the sole unverified hop in the causal chain.
- Log-vs-counter arithmetic is unresolved: 360 logged trade scans over ~121 minutes (~3/min) vs a ~140/min counter plateau (~46x). Because slow-query logging captures only executions above the threshold, true trade-scan execution rate is unknown, and no tool exposes total executions.
- Blast radius was not measured: no latency/error/db-pool metrics were retrieved for portfolio-service, payment-service, settlement-service or inventory-service (tool budget exhausted), so it is unproven whether other ledger-db co-tenants were degraded and unproven that the impact was confined to order-service+ledger-db.
- Change-data completeness is not proven: get_deployments and get_config_changes each returned only one row even after widening to 5-8 days, both with partial:false; a migration applied directly to ledger-db or a connection-limit/pool change not recorded in this source cannot be ruled out.
- Sub-bucket ordering is unresolvable: metric buckets are 360-420s, so the connections vs CPU vs slow-query sequence within 08:54-09:00Z, and the 42-second ordering of the 08:56Z deploy vs the metric bucket, cannot be established; only the log ordering (08:58:38Z trade scan -> 09:00:42Z order timeout) is verified.
- ledger-db has no lock-wait/bloat metric and autovacuum activity appears only as INFO log lines, so any lock or vacuum contribution from cfg-2903 cannot be quantified or excluded beyond the temporal argument.
- Log evidence is truncated (ledger-db 410 matched / 40 returned; order-service 288 matched / 40 returned) and ledger-db log retention starts ~05:00Z, so 00:00Z-05:00Z has no log coverage and later-window non-trades slow-query species cannot be fully excluded.
- The segmented trade-scan rate was only measured for 09:00-09:40Z; slices for the 08:56-09:00Z onset bucket and beyond 09:40Z were not executed (budget exhausted), so the exact onset-bucket rate and the later-window rate are unmeasured.

## What would change this conclusion
- Evidence that the 'Seq Scan on trades' query existed and was executing at comparable rate before 08:56Z (e.g. pre-deploy log lines in 05:00-08:56Z or a per-statement counter showing it running at ~140/min beforehand) would break the deploy-boundary correlation and undermine H1.
- A measured per-application split showing the ~140/min slow-query counter and ~190 connections are dominated by order-service's own INSERT load or by another tenant/autovacuum, rather than by portfolio-service's trades query, would demote H1 in favor of H3-style aggregate saturation.

- Connection-term evidence that ledger-db connections were being consumed by a pool/connection leak in order-service or another tenant (e.g. per-application connection counts or pg_stat_activity samples) would shift the load source away from the trades query.
- Proof that the trades query had an effective index or was bounded (e.g. a schema listing showing an index on trades(account_id, executed_at) in use) would contradict the unindexed-seq-scan mechanism of H1.
- Evidence that portfolio-service's query volume and latency were normal after 08:56Z while ledger-db still saturated would decouple the deploy from the DB load and point to a different origin.
- Discovery of an unrecorded change straddling 08:55Z (a migration applied directly to ledger-db, a connection-limit or pool-sizing change) would introduce a competing origin that the current change source did not capture.
