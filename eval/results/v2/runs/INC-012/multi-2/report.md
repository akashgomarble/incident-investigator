# Incident investigation report: INC-012
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
The dominant cause of INC-012 is storage exhaustion at the shared ledger-db datastore: the /var/lib/postgresql/data volume filled monotonically (94.73% at 14:00Z -> 98% at 14:48:20Z -> pinned 100.0 from the 14:56Z bucket) driven by WAL being retained rather than recycled by the inactive 'analytics_cdc' replication slot, which was already announcing tens of GB of retained WAL from 13:02:25Z. PostgreSQL then began PANICing on WAL writes ('could not write to file pg_wal/xlogtemp... No space left on device') from 15:01:01Z, and every write path that reaches ledger-db fails with the identical ENOSPC signature — payment-service's 354 'ledger write failed' ERROR lines (first 15:00:26Z) and order-service's independent 'INSERT INTO orders failed' lines, which no payment-service code fault can explain. payment-service v6.0.0 (dep-6203, 14:46:00Z) is the only change in the window but is not the ongoing cause: it was rolled back at 15:12:00Z, after which the error rate did not step down and the disk stayed pinned at 100% with PANICs continuing. Confidence in this root cause is medium (0.70): the fault and the fix target (reclaim the volume and restore WAL recycling) are well verified, but the origin of the slot inactivity is unobservable in this telemetry.

## Probable root cause
**ledger-db volume exhausted by uncontrolled WAL retention; storage ENOSPC is the dominant ongoing cause (deploy neither necessary nor sufficient)** -- resource_exhaustion in `ledger-db`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.70, medium)

### H1: ledger-db volume exhausted by uncontrolled WAL retention; storage ENOSPC is the dominant ongoing cause (deploy neither necessary nor sufficient)
The ledger-db datastore exhausted its /var/lib/postgresql/data volume. Disk climbed steadily and monotonically (94.73% at 14:00Z -> 97.85% at 14:35Z -> 98% at 14:45-14:48Z -> pinned 100.0 from the 14:56Z bucket to end of window), and PostgreSQL began PANICing ('could not extend file' / 'could not write to file pg_wal/xlogtemp: No space left on device') from 15:01:01Z through 16:57:21Z. Because the storage layer cannot allocate, every write that grows a relation fails with ENOSPC: payment-service's ledger writes (354 identical ERROR lines) and order-service's independent 'INSERT INTO orders' writes both fail. The fill is driven by WAL that is being retained rather than recycled; the ledger-db log repeatedly reports 'replication slot ... is inactive; retaining <n> MB of WAL' (5 WARN lines from 13:02:25Z through 14:49:47Z, with retained-MB values oscillating in the tens of GB the whole time), so a slot with no managed/known consumer is the identified retention source. The precise instant retention began and the consumer-side cause are NOT observable in this telemetry (no slot-state or WAL-bytes metric exists; the 'analytics_cdc' consumer has no catalog entry). payment-service v6.0.0 (dep-6203, 14:46Z) is the only change of any kind across all services in the window; it is not the ongoing cause (rollback at 15:12Z changed nothing), but it is not positively exonerated as a transient write/WAL-generation contributor to an already-climbing fill. Fix target: reclaim the ledger-db volume and restore WAL recycling / drop the orphaned slot — not payment-service code.
