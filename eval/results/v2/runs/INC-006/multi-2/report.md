# Incident investigation report: INC-006
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
Root cause identified with medium confidence (0.70): the shared redis-cache lost its primary at 13:22:10Z and promoted a replica with an empty dataset, causing a cluster-wide cache-miss storm in which market-data-service rebuilt quotes from the exchange feed (6864ms), inflating its p99 from a flat ~40ms to ~1.85-1.98s in one minute and pushing pricing-engine past its quote-computation deadline, surfacing as 2-3s quotes on trading screens. The downstream chain (market-data-service -> pricing-engine -> screens) is near-certain and well evidenced; the origin is the shared cache rather than a market-data-service-local ceiling because a second redis-cache consumer on an unrelated path (portfolio-service) degraded in the same 13:21 bucket, market-data-service's only onset-window log is an explicit cache-miss message with no pool/retry/GC/timeout messages, and the cache hit ratio collapsed from ~96% to 12.32% with flat evictions. Alternatives are contradicted by current-window evidence: the exchange hop is flat on every signal (H2), the only deployment predates onset by ~110 minutes and the deployed service is itself unaffected (H3), and H4's local CPU ceiling is equally well explained as the cost of miss-recompute and cannot explain portfolio-service. Residual uncertainty: redis-cache exposes no latency metric, so a slow-but-alive primary in the minutes before 13:22:10Z cannot be excluded, and the empty-replica inference rests on hit-ratio behavior rather than directly observed dataset size.

## Probable root cause
**redis-cache primary failover / empty replica → cluster-wide miss storm → market-data-service slow → pricing-engine deadline exceeded** -- infrastructure_failure in `redis-cache`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.70, medium)

### H1: redis-cache primary failover / empty replica → cluster-wide miss storm → market-data-service slow → pricing-engine deadline exceeded
redis-cache, the SHARED cache used by market-data-service, pricing-engine, auth-service and portfolio-service, lost its primary and promoted a replica with an EMPTY dataset at 13:22:10-13:22:52Z. Hit ratio collapsed ~96% → 39.33% (13:21 bucket) → 12.32% (13:24), a miss storm consistent with an empty promoted replica; evictions stayed flat, so not memory-pressure eviction. Read-through misses forced market-data-service to rebuild quotes from the exchange feed ('cache miss storm: quote:<n> not in redis-cache, rebuilding from exchange feed snapshot (6864ms)'), inflating its p99 from a flat ~40ms to ~1.85-1.98s in a single minute (13:21 → 13:22) while its request_rate stayed flat — the CPU jump to 86-94% is the cost of that recompute, not an autonomous ceiling. market-data-service logged no ERRORs (silent latency failure). pricing-engine's quote computation then exceeded its deadline against market-data-service: 192 errors, all 'market-data-service took <n>ms' (827-8456ms), p99 60.87ms → 2225.2ms, surfacing as 2-3s quotes on trading screens. The round-2 data removed the earlier timing objection: market-data-service did NOT lead — its true 1-minute p99 series is flat <43ms through 13:21 and steps at 13:22, the same minute as the redis-cache failover, and the 13:16 'degradation' was a bucket-averaging artifact. The scope test is now answered constructively: portfolio-service, a second shared-redis-cache consumer on an unrelated path, degraded at the same onset (182.2ms → 667.29ms at 13:21, ~715-888ms thereafter, latency-only with flat error rate), while the shared cache's other resource metrics show no CPU saturation — a shared-cache-origin fingerprint.

### H2: External exchange-gateway/exchange-api slowdown propagating up through market-data-service

## Data gaps
- redis-cache exposes no latency metric at all (available_metrics: connected_clients, cpu_pct, evictions_per_min, hit_ratio_pct, memory_pct), so cache-side latency degradation can never be proven directly — it must be inferred from hit ratio and client-side logs (F29).
- Whether the redis-cache primary was slow-but-alive for minutes before the 13:22:10Z down event cannot be excluded; a check of the failover-event archive / post-failover cache warm-up history is required.
- Sub-minute ordering between the redis-cache failover (13:22:10Z, 130s into the 13:22 bucket) and market-data-service's 13:22 latency step is unresolvable at 1-minute granularity, so the cache-first ordering is assumed from the absence of any pre-13:22 market-data-service degradation rather than observed.
- The empty-replica inference rests on hit-ratio behavior and flat evictions rather than a directly observed dataset size on the promoted replica.
- The first get_deployments call returned partial:true with a warning that one or more backend shards did not respond; the successful retry returned only 1 deployment, but a shard-level omission of deployments (e.g. a market-data-service or redis-cache change) cannot be fully excluded.
- Config/flag changes are often untracked and no per-component flag registry was queried, so ruling out a config cause (e.g. redis TTL/eviction or timeout settings) is not fully supported despite a count of 0.
- market-data-service exposes no connection-pool, thread-pool, GC-pause or disk-I/O metrics, so a non-CPU local ceiling — the origin H4 actually names — remains untested in either direction.
- market-data-service log coverage is very sparse (3 INFO entries in 13:00-13:22:24), so absence of a cache-miss message before 13:22:25 does not prove no cache-miss occurred at the 13:22 metric onset; log-to-metric onset offset could not be closed.
- redis-cache memory_pct and cpu_pct over the window were partially retrieved (cpu_pct flat ~22% at onset, F36) but memory_pct was not, so memory pressure as the cause of the primary's failure is not fully excluded, though flat evictions argue against it (F27).
- auth-service's flatness at onset is unexplained; it could reflect a genuinely different cache access pattern rather than the absence of a shared-cache impact, so the scope test is suggestive but not decisive about why auth-service was spared.
- No quote-serving edge service was identified (query for 'quote-service' returned not_found), so the full blast radius on the quote path (e.g. api-gateway, order-service) is unverified.

## What would change this conclusion
- Evidence that market-data-service's p99 or cache-miss activity was elevated before 13:22:10Z (e.g. a 13:16-13:21 cache-miss log or a redis-cache hit-ratio drop before the 13:22 failover) would break the cache-first ordering and favor a market-data-service-local origin (H4).
- A verified redis-cache latency or dataset-size metric showing no change at 13:22 — or evidence that the promoted replica was fully populated — would undermine the empty-replica mechanism, though the hit-ratio collapse (96% -> 12.32%) currently argues strongly against this.

- Any exchange-gateway or exchange-api latency/error movement at 13:16-13:22 (currently contradicted by flat p99, error rate, upstream latency/error and logs) would revive H2.
- Discovery, via the failed-shard deployment inventory, of a change to market-data-service, redis-cache or its infra close to 13:21-13:22 would redirect the origin away from the shared-cache loss event.
- Evidence that portfolio-service's 13:21 latency jump was caused by something other than redis-cache (e.g. a coincident portfolio-service-local load event) would remove the strongest constructive discriminator between shared-cache and market-data-service-local origin.
