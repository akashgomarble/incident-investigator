# Incident investigation report: INC-005
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
Root cause identified with medium confidence (0.68, capped by the Critic): payment-service's mTLS client certificate CN=payments-client.orangeinvest.internal expired at 2026-04-02T12:15:00Z, and every subsequent outbound call to bank-partner-api was rejected with TLS alert 45 (expired certificate). Onset is pinned to a single bucket — bank_api_error_rate stepped 0.2%→100% and payment_success_rate collapsed 99.7%→2.5% at 12:15:00Z — and all 311 payment-service ERRORs carry the identical expired-client-cert signature naming our own cert and notAfter stamp. The dashboard stayed green because the error_rate metric only counts HTTP 5xx while failed transfers are marked PENDING_RETRY internally (failures occur on the bank egress, outside the synchronous request path). The origin is localized to payment-service's own client-credential lifecycle: no renewal artifact (no cert-manager/secret entity, zero payment-service deployments, no cert-related config change) exists anywhere in the change feed, and no sibling service shows a matching TLS/bank symptom. Two residual details remain unfalsifiable with available telemetry (never-renewed vs. renewed-but-not-reloaded; a coincident bank-side event), carried as assumptions.

## What would change this conclusion
- A valid, unexpired client certificate found in payment-service's TLS secret with notAfter after 12:15:00Z on 2026-04-02 would indicate the cert was renewed and the fault is a stale TLS context / missing reload, shifting the fix to a config-reload/rollout (origin service unchanged).
- Evidence of a bank-partner-api-side outage or 5xx/refused-handshake episode at exactly 12:15:00Z — e.g. vendor incident notice or a matching caller-side 5xx signature — would support H2 (external dependency failure) over H1.
- Discovery that settlement-service, order-service or portfolio-service also stepped to 100% bank_api_error_rate at exactly 12:15:00Z, or that they share the same client cert/notAfter, would indicate a shared CA/secret-issuer failure rather than payment-service's own credential lifecycle.
- A payment-service pod restart or TLS-context reload event coinciding with renewal would establish the 'renewed-but-not-reloaded' sub-origin and change the immediate remediaction from cert issuance to a forced reload/rollout.
- A verified bank_api_call latency increase or partial failure on the bank edge before 12:15:00Z (if bank_api_latency_ms existed) would indicate partial degradation rather than a hard, total reject at the cert notAfter instant.
- Evidence that the failures were not TLS-related at all — e.g. server-side logs showing a different error class or confirming the failures were client-side — would overturn H1 in favour of H5.
