*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
The top hypothesis H1 is confirmed with high confidence (0.86): the shared redis-cache primary failed its PING health checks at 13:22:10Z (F37), and failover promoted redis-cache-3, a replica that was still resyncing and therefore held an empty dataset (F37/F38). With the quote:<id> keyspace gone, hit_ratio collapsed 96.34%->10.45% and memory_pct 74.63%->6.0% in the same minute, with evictions flat and no maxmemory ceiling or flush marker (F19/F20/F22/F34/F35/F41). That forced market-data-service into a cold-rebuild miss storm from 13:22:25Z (F16/F32), stepping its p99 from ~40ms to 1850ms (F31/F33), which pushed pricing-engine past its quote computation deadline — p99 60ms->2225ms with a single error signature naming market-data-service while pricing-engine CPU stayed flat ~39-40% (F13/F14/F17/F18). All alternatives are contradicted by measured evidence, not merely dismissed: H2 by flat MDS request_rate (F36), H3 by flat exchange-gateway/upstream metrics (F25-F30), H4 by the dependency graph and a ~1h52m gap (F3/F5), and H5 by two non-partial zero-change queries (F2/F8/F9). Two residual details cap confidence at 0.86 rather than blocking: the trigger of the PING timeouts is unidentified, and the blast-radius asymmetry (order-service flat while api-gateway latency-steps) is unexplained — neither names a different origin.

### H1: Shared redis-cache primary failover promoted an empty (resyncing) replica, causing a quote-key miss storm that starved market-data-service and blew pricing-engine's quote deadline
At ~13:22 the shared redis-cache layer lost its entire dataset. The mechanism is now directly observed, not inferred: at 13:22:10Z ERROR 'node redis-cache-2 failed health check (3 consecutive PING timeouts); marking primary as down', followed at 13:22:52Z by WARN 'failover: promoting replica redis-cache-3 to primary; dataset on new primary is empty (replica was resyncing)' (F37). So the primary failed its health checks and the promoted replica was still resyncing — it held no quote keyspace — which is exactly the emptied-dataset signature seen in telemetry: hit_ratio collapsed 96.34%->10.45% and memory_pct 74.63%->6.0% in the same 13:22 bucket, with evictions flat ~2-4 keys/min and no maxmemory ceiling, and no FLUSHALL/FLUSHDB/config-reset marker in any redis log (F19/F20/F34/F35/F22/F41). With the quote:<id> keyspace gone, market-data-service's cache path became a cold rebuild from the exchange feed snapshot for every quote ('cache miss storm' WARNs from 13:22:25Z, rebuild values up to 6864-8456ms; F16/F32), driving MDS p99 from ~40ms (13:21 flat 36.7-41.8ms) to 1850.54ms in the 13:22 bucket and error_rate 0.13%->3.2% in that same bucket (F31/F33). Pricing-engine, whose only quote path is MDS, then exceeded its computation deadline — p99 60ms->2225.2ms at 13:22, error_rate ~0.2%->4.18%, single error signature 'quote computation deadline exceeded: market-data-service took <n>ms' (827-8456ms) — with its own CPU flat at ~39-40% and request rate unchanged, so the blowup is wait-time on the callee, not local compute (F13/F14/F17/F18). Origin is the shared cache node failing over to an empty replica, with no deployment, rollback, or config/feature-flag change anywhere in the window (F1/F2/F8/F9). Same mechanism as precedent HIST-107 'Cold cache storm after redis failover' on redis-cache + market-data-service (F6/F11/F12).

- redis-cache exposes no keyspace/key-count and no expired_keys metric (only connected_clients, cpu_pct, evictions_per_min, hit_ratio_pct, memory_pct), so the literal quote:<id> keyspace-shrink and expired_keys checks could not be performed; dataset loss is proven via hit_ratio/memory_pct collapse plus the observed empty-replica promotion.
- The redis-cache logs do not state why redis-cache-2's PINGs timed out (no OOM, crash, or explicit cause line), so crash vs network partition vs host fault remains unresolved.
- Wall-clock ordering is exact for logs but only minute-bucketed for metrics: p99 and error-rate onsets are bracketed to the 13:22:00-13:23:00 bucket for redis-cache, market-data-service, pricing-engine and api-gateway, so metric onset cannot be resolved to sub-minute precision relative to the 13:22:10Z/13:22:25Z log events.

## What would change this conclusion
- Evidence that redis-cache-3 was actually fully synced at promotion and that a different event emptied the dataset (e.g. a redis log line or config-reset marker showing a deliberate flush or write-pattern reset) would break the specific failover-to-empty-replica mechanism and push the conclusion toward a different loss cause.
- Finding that the quote:<id> keyspace was still present after 13:22 (measurable if a keyspace/key-count or DBSIZE metric were exposed) would contradict key loss and shift the origin back to a rebuild-path or exchange-feed cause.
- Demonstration that exchange-gateway/upstream latency or error rose before 13:22 in a resolution finer than the bucket average, or that snapshot-rebuild latency rose independently of key absence, would revive H3 (slow snapshot-rebuild path).
- Evidence of a rise in market-data-service ingress volume or its CPU saturation at 13:22 (it is currently flat at 910-1026 req/s and MDS CPU was never retrieved for that bucket) would revive H2 (capacity saturation) as a contributing or alternative cause.
- Discovery of an unrecorded deployment, feature-flag toggle, or routing/traffic-shift affecting pricing-engine or market-data-service at ~13:22 would reopen H5 and contradict the zero-change finding.
- Finding a code/config change in auth-service that demonstrably alters the pricing-engine request path (a coupling outside the documented dependency graph) would reopen H4.
- If the redis-cache events at 13:22:10Z/13:22:52Z turned out to be unrelated background events (e.g. promotion to a replica that did hold data), the causal chain would lose its first hop and the conclusion would fall back to inconclusive.
