# Incident investigation report: INC-009
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
Root cause (high confidence, 0.85): dep-5905 — portfolio-service v4.5.0, deployed 2026-05-05T08:56:00Z — added a "trades filtered by account_id + executed_at" query with no accompanying migration/index; on the shared ledger-db it executes as a Seq Scan over ~48.2M rows, driving a one-minute step at 08:58:00Z in ledger-db slow queries (1.33 → 139.17/min), active connections (60.10 → 185.11) and CPU (30.53% → 95.99%, sustained 95–97%) with disk_used_pct and replication_lag_ms flat — i.e. CPU-bound query saturation, not IO. That contention blocked order-service's "INSERT INTO orders" work (pool in use 14 → 46), producing the 5s p99 and statement timeouts from 09:00:42Z. The competing autovacuum config change cfg-2903 (08:00Z) shows no CPU or slow-query effect across the following 58 minutes and its scale_factor mechanism cannot produce a one-minute cliff; infrastructure/IO and order-service-side resource causes are contradicted by flat disk/replication metrics and zero order-service deployments. Scope evidence is decisive: the changed service degraded ~26x at onset while the two other ledger-db co-tenants stayed flat within ~2%.

## Probable root cause
**portfolio-service dep-5905 added an unindexed trades query; seq scans saturated shared ledger-db CPU, blocking order-service INSERTs** -- bad_deployment in `portfolio-service`, triggered by `dep-5905` (confidence 0.85, high)

### H1: portfolio-service dep-5905 added an unindexed trades query; seq scans saturated shared ledger-db CPU, blocking order-service INSERTs
portfolio-service v4.5.0 (dep-5905) added 'SELECT * FROM trades WHERE account_id=$1 AND executed_at > $2 ORDER BY executed_at' with no migration/index. On the shared pg ledger-db this degrades to a Seq Scan over ~48M rows. At the single 08:58:00Z minute, ledger-db slow_queries_per_min jumps 1.33 -> 139.17, connections_active 60.10 -> 185.11, and cpu_pct 30.53 -> 95.99 (single-step, sustained ~95-97%), while disk_used_pct and replication_lag_ms stay flat -- i.e. CPU saturation driven by query load, not IO. This contention blocks order-service's 'INSERT INTO orders' work (pool in use 14 -> 46), producing statement timeouts and 5s p99. Decisive scope evidence: the query's own service (portfolio-service) rose ~26x at onset (185ms -> 4701ms), the two other ledger-db co-tenants (payment-service, settlement-service) stayed flat within ~2%, showing the impact is specific to the changed workload rather than uniform DB saturation.
