# Incident investigation report: INC-008
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
Login slowness and 503s on auth-service (p99 ~90ms -> ~3.77s, 5xx ~0.2% -> ~14%) were caused by an inbound traffic surge that overwhelmed auth-service's fixed admission capacity, not by the bcrypt regression the reporter suspected. An ~3.8-3.9x traffic step starting in the 06:47-06:50 UTC buckets is visible at the api-gateway boundary and carried into auth-service as a stable ~37% subset; auth-service's worker pool filled (64/64, queues to 98763), the autoscaler could not exceed maxReplicas 8, and auth-service itself emitted "accept queue full" 503s. Confidence in H1 (capacity saturation) is 0.62 (medium); the mechanism and scope are well supported, with the residual uncertainty being whether the surge was genuine external demand or a client/retry storm (H5, 0.20) - both share the same mechanism and the same primary mitigation. All change-based causes were tested and excluded: no auth-service deployment in 25 hours, no bcrypt/log signature, redis-cache flat on all five metrics, and user-db connection growth follows rather than precedes onset.

## Probable root cause
**Incoming login traffic surge overwhelms auth-service's fixed admission capacity (mechanism established; origin external/non-change-driven)** -- capacity_saturation in `auth-service`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.62, medium)

### H1: Incoming login traffic surge overwhelms auth-service's fixed admission capacity (mechanism established; origin external/non-change-driven)
Incoming request volume to the login path stepped up ~3.8-4.5x beginning in the 06:47-06:50 UTC buckets and auth-service could not absorb it. Its fixed worker pool (64/64) filled, the accept queue grew into the tens of thousands (peaks 98763), CPU pinned near 100%, and the autoscaler could not help because desired replicas (14) exceeded the configured maxReplicas (8). The saturated admission path itself returned 503, producing p99 ~3.8s and ~14% 5xx. This requires no auth-service code, config or resource change: no deployment or config change maps to onset (F1-F3, F16, F37-F40). The surge is not an auth-service-only internal echo — api-gateway request_rate rose ~3.9x on the identical bucket grid (F31, F32, F33) and the gateway's own error_rate reached only ~half of auth-service's, consistent with auth-service originating the 5xx (F35). The remaining open question is the surge's origin (external demand vs client loop), treated separately in H5.

**Causal chain**
1. Inbound request volume steps ~3.8x starting in the 06:47-06:50 buckets: auth-service 394 -> ~1.49k req/s and api-gateway ~1.2k -> 4.79k req/s on the identical bucket grid [F12, F16, F31, F32, F44]
2. auth-service rate is a stable ~37% subset of the gateway's volume throughout, so this is not a multiplied internal echo contained inside auth-service [F33, F47]
3. auth-service worker pool saturates (active=64/64, queue 39035, peaks 98763) with CPU pinned ~100% [F11]
4. The autoscaler cannot relieve pressure: desired 14 replicas > maxReplicas 8, so no scale-out occurs [F12]
5. auth-service itself rejects requests at admission with 'accept queue full' 503s on POST /v1/auth/login; its own logs show no bcrypt/hash errors and zero downstream timeout/DB errors [F10, F14, F15]
6. Resulting symptom: p99 jumps from ~90ms to ~3.77s and 5xx rises from 0.2% to ~13.8-14.1% [F8, F9]
7. The gateway's error_rate rises only to ~6-9% (~half of auth-service's ~14%), indicating the 5xx originate at auth-service rather than at the gateway [F35, F45]
8. No service outside the gateway->auth-service path degrades: pricing-engine, market-data-service, portfolio-service request_rate/latency_p99/error_rate are flat through 06:47-06:52 [F48, F49, F50, F51, F52]

### H5: Retry loop / client storm amplifies traffic into the login path (shared multiplier), saturating auth-service
Rather than genuine new demand, a misbehaving client, client-side retry loop, or upstream retry amplification multiplied request volume entering through api-gateway and terminating at auth-service's /v1/auth/login, overwhelming auth-service's fixed admission capacity (the H1 mechanism). This is a genuinely different ORIGIN than H1: no new external demand, just multiplied retries. It matters because the fix differs — block the offending client / fix the retry loop rather than raise maxReplicas. The findings cannot confirm this: the gateway exposes no client/IP attribution, no per-route request rate, and no retries or upstream-5xx counter (F36, F43), and the ~37% auth-service-to-gateway ratio (F33) is consistent with a shared multiplier scaling both series proportionally.

**Causal chain**
1. A client/upstream retry multiplier inflates volume at the gateway boundary, driving both gateway and auth-service request_rate up proportionally *(assumed)*
2. auth-service's rate remains a stable ~37% subset of the gateway's, consistent with a shared retry multiplier as well as with genuine demand [F33, F47]
3. The amplified volume saturates auth-service's fixed worker pool and accepted-queue, yielding 'accept queue full' 503s and p99 ~3.8s [F10, F11, F12, F8, F9]

**Supporting evidence**

### H4: user-db connection-pool / query pressure is the first mover and slows auth-service logins

**Causal chain**
1. user-db connections_active steps ~3x from ~24-26 to ~72-82 in the 06:52 UTC one-minute bucket [F13, F26]
2. user-db query latency / pool wait rises, so auth-service logins block on DB calls and hold worker slots *(assumed)*
3. Queue backlog yields 503 accept-queue rejections and p99 ~3.8s [F10, F8]

**Supporting evidence**

### H3: Shared redis-cache (co-tenant) degradation propagates into auth-service login path
The shared redis-cache used by auth-service, portfolio-service, pricing-engine and market-data-service degraded (e.g. eviction / hit-ratio / memory pressure driven by the 05:50 portfolio-service 'Holdings export' release), slowing session/token lookups inside logins and backing up auth-service's workers.

**Causal chain**
1. portfolio-service v4.4.0 dep-5803 (CSV 'Holdings export') at 05:50 adds load to the shared redis-cache [F1, F5]
2. redis-cache degrades in a way that slows auth-service session/token lookups *(assumed)*
3. slower logins back up auth-service workers, producing queue saturation and 503s [F11, F10]

**Supporting evidence**
  - `F5` ✔ SHARED COMPONENT: redis-cache is a shared cache consumed by four independent services -- auth-service, portfolio-service, pricing-engine, market-data-service -- all with kind 'cache'. So a redis-cache degradation or a cache-pressure event driven by the OTHER tenants (e.g. the 05:50 portfolio-service v4.4.0 'Holdings export' release) can reach auth-service's login path through this shared dependency.
  - `F7` ✔ Precedent caution: the shared redis-cache has its own login-adjacent incident history -- HIST-107 (2025-12-09 'Cold cache storm after redis failover', services [redis-cache, market-data-service], symptoms 'cache miss storm','quote latency', root cause 'Redis primary failure promoted an empty replica'). This is a lead only: similar-shaped symptoms in past incidents had infrastructure causes as well as bad-deployment causes (BTC-110 IOPS throttling, HIST-113 CPU saturation).
**Contradicting evidence**
