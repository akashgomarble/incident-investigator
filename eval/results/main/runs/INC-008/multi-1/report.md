# Incident investigation report: INC-008
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
Top hypothesis H4 (medium, 0.60): an inbound login-traffic surge (~4x) from outside the platform exceeded auth-service's provisioned capacity ceiling, saturating its fixed 64-worker pool and its maxReplicas=8 autoscaler cap, filling the accept queue and producing 503s on POST /v1/auth/login with p99 stepping ~40x to ~3.75-4.0s at 06:54Z. The mechanism and demand-side driver are verified on telemetry: request_rate rose from ~394 to ~1642 req/s starting in the 06:48-06:51Z interval, rate_limited_per_min exploded ~400x, CPU saturated at 96-98%, worker queues reached ~94k, the autoscaler was blocked at maxReplicas 8, and the only 503 signature in logs is "accept queue full ... (POST /v1/auth/login)". The reporter's bcrypt-regression hypothesis (H2, rejected) is excluded: zero auth-service deployments in the window or prior week, no config/flag change near onset, and no bcrypt/timeout/OOM log signature; the shared-redis-cache hypothesis (H3, rejected) is excluded by flat cache CPU/connections/evictions and latency-flat co-tenants. Residual uncertainty is confined to the origin sub-hop (external flood vs original-caller retry) and login-endpoint confinement, both of which the platform's metric layer cannot measure; neither changes the mechanism or the immediate mitigation.

## Probable root cause
**Inbound login traffic surge (external flood / credential-stuffing) overwhelms auth-service capacity ceiling** -- capacity_saturation in `auth-service`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.60, medium)

### H4: Inbound login traffic surge (external flood / credential-stuffing) overwhelms auth-service capacity ceiling
An inbound request surge driven from outside the platform (~4x, appearing simultaneously at api-gateway and auth-service) exceeded auth-service's provisioned capacity. With a fixed 64-worker pool and maxReplicas 8, the service hit its ceiling: CPU saturated (~97%), worker queues grew to ~94k, the accept queue filled, and POST /v1/auth/login was rejected with 503s while p99 stepped ~40x to ~3.75-4.0s. Rate limiting exploded ~400x. No code, config or dependency change near onset; memory and restarts normal.

**Causal chain**
1. An external flood starts: api-gateway request_rate and auth-service request_rate rise in the SAME 120s bucket (gateway 1227.99->1500.82, auth 386.14->452.43 at 06:50Z), with comparable ~4-4.7x rise factors, so the increase is not amplified downstream of the gateway. [F38, F39, F43, F24]
2. Auth-service inbound rate climbs from ~394 req/s baseline to 671.71 (06:51Z), 1154.93 (06:54Z), peaking 1641.69 avg / 1813.74 max (06:57Z), holding ~1400-1550 req/s. [F20, F46]
3. Load is far beyond provisioned capacity and actively rejected: rate_limited_per_min jumps from ~1.5-2.3/min to 607.72 (06:54Z) and ~885-916/min sustained (~400x). [F21, F47]
4. Auth-service CPU climbs from ~36-40% to saturation ~96-98% from 06:54Z onward; CPU saturation precedes/coincides with latency and error onset. [F10]
5. Worker pool saturates (active=64/64, queue=39035, queues reaching ~94k) from 06:54:53Z; autoscaler desired 14 exceeds maxReplicas 8 and cannot scale. [F12]
6. Accept queue full -> 503 rejections, whose only error signature is 'request rejected: accept queue full ... (POST /v1/auth/login)' (372 occurrences); 5xx rises from ~0.1-0.32% to ~11.7-14.4%. [F11, F9, F48]
7. p99 steps ~40x from ~82-97 ms to ~3.75-4.0s in the same 06:54Z bucket; api-gateway shows the same 06:54Z step (error_rate to ~9%, p99 to ~3.87s), carrying the excess load. [F8, F33, F34, F36]
8. Alternatives excluded: memory stays 48-57%, restarts 0, no deployment within 64 min of onset, no config/flag change near onset. [F22, F23, F14, F15, F16, F17]

- The login-filtered ERROR query was truncated (104 matches, 20 returned), and cfg-2801 suppressed INFO per-route request logs on auth-service, so no request-path logs exist to reconstruct which endpoints received the excess volume.
- Sibling backends (portfolio-service, pricing-engine, market-data-service) were proven latency-flat but their own HTTP 5xx error rates in the window were not retrieved, so 'no errors on siblings' is not directly evidenced.

## What would change this conclusion
- Source-of-traffic data showing the ~4x surge originates from upstream callers or the platform's own retrying components (rather than an external source) would move the conclusion from an external inbound flood toward caller-driven retry amplification, changing the mitigation from edge blocking to fixing the amplifying caller.
- A measured per-endpoint breakdown showing the ~4x rise is service-wide across all auth-service endpoints rather than concentrated on POST /v1/auth/login would weaken the credential-stuffing reading of the surge (though not the capacity-saturation mechanism).
- Evidence of a redis-cache-side degradation or of one of the shared co-tenants (portfolio-service, pricing-engine, market-data-service) degrading at or before 06:54Z would reopen the shared-cache hypothesis H3, which is currently contradicted by flat cache CPU/connections/evictions and latency-flat co-tenants.
- Discovery of an unrecorded auth-path change near onset (a feature-flag flip, schema migration, or job change outside the deployment/config systems) that alters crypto cost or request handling would reopen H2, which is currently excluded by zero auth-service deployments in-window and prior week, no config/flag change near onset, and no bcrypt/timeout/OOM log signature.
- Evidence of kyc-provider stalls (or of a synchronous kyc-provider call on the login path with no timeout) would raise H5 from untestable floor to a live mechanism, since that dependency is unobservable from this platform by construction.
- Evidence of memory pressure, restarts, or OOM behavior on auth-service would contradict the demand-driven saturation mechanism; these are currently excluded by memory staying at 48-57% and restarts at 0.
- Evidence that auth-service inbound request volume exceeded or preceded api-gateway-forwarded volume in any aligned bucket would indicate retry/duplicate amplification at the gateway, contradicting the same-bucket matching-rise finding (F38/F40/F43).
