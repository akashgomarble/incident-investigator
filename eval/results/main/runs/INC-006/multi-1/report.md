# Incident investigation report: INC-006
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
The quote-latency incident INC-006 was caused by a failover of the shared redis-cache to an empty, still-resyncing replica, which triggered a cold-cache miss storm that degraded both pricing-engine and market-data-service (H1, confidence 0.78, high). The cache's hit ratio collapsed from ~96% to 12.86% and memory from ~70% to 7.25% in the 13:18Z-13:24Z buckets while evictions and CPU stayed flat, and explicit redis-cache logs show redis-cache-2 marked down at 13:22:10Z with empty replica redis-cache-3 promoted at 13:22:52Z. market-data-service then logged a cache-miss storm rebuilding quotes from the exchange feed (1034-8748ms) starting 13:22:25Z, and pricing-engine's only error signature was a downstream deadline exceeded on market-data-service; market-data-service CPU rose only after latency, marking it as a consequence rather than the cause. The blast radius maps exactly onto shared-cache consumers (pricing-engine, market-data-service, api-gateway as pass-through, plus non-quote-path portfolio-service), while non-cache controls stayed flat and no deploy or config change exists anywhere on the quote path, so capacity saturation (H2), a bad deployment (H3/H4) and external vendor degradation (H5) are affirmatively contradicted.

## Probable root cause
**Shared redis-cache failover to an empty replica -> cold-cache miss storm degrades both pricing-engine and market-data-service** -- infrastructure_failure in `redis-cache`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.78, high)

## Data gaps
- redis-cache permanently lacks latency_p99_ms/error_rate, uptime/restart, last_save/rdb_last_bgsave and raw keyspace_hits/keyspace_misses counters (F37/F42/F47) — the cache's own response time and restart/flush event cannot be cross-checked at the metric layer; only the failover service-event log establishes the event.
- exchange-api (vendor boundary at the foot of the quote path) has has_telemetry:false (F23), so external vendor health is observable only indirectly through exchange-gateway metrics; H5 cannot be positively excluded, only contradicted at the measured boundary (F34/F36).
- market-data-service exposes no worker/connection utilisation metric (F33), so worker/connection saturation on the callee could not be assessed; its CPU-after-latency ordering (F31/F35) is the available substitute.
- portfolio-service's direct portfolio-service->redis-cache edge was not fetched, and its request_rate/cpu_pct queries hit budget_exhausted — the mechanism of its latency step (cache-miss wait vs. load) and the claim that it is a shared-cache consumer remain inferential.
- Latency/error for additional candidate services (risk-engine, user-service, kafka and other redis-cache consumers) was not fetched, so the 'non-cache consumers stayed flat' control set is limited to order-service, auth-service and notification-service (F45) and a full enumeration of redis-cache consumers is unavailable.
- The config-change scans returned a completely empty result and coverage of a separate feature-flag provider was not independently confirmable; a flag toggle outside the connector's scope is weakened but not bulletproof-excluded (F20/F21).
- Metrics are 300s buckets, so the onset could not be pinned below 5-minute resolution and the exact sub-bucket order between cache emptiness and the service step relies on logs, not metrics.
- No deployment history before 2026-04-09T00:00Z was compared as a baseline for order-service/api-gateway; such a change would predate onset by >13h and is not mechanistically likely.

## What would change this conclusion
- Evidence that redis-cache's hit_ratio/memory collapse was a metric artifact or a benign scrape/reset rather than real data loss — e.g. a corresponding keyspace counter showing the dataset intact — would remove the mechanism behind H1 (partly unverifiable: no keyspace counters exist).
- A redis-cache log or metric showing the cache remained populated and served low-latency reads throughout 13:18-13:24Z (i.e. no real failover/emptying) would point back to H2, capacity saturation on market-data-service, or an as-yet-unidentified origin.
- A market-data-service-local resource or code fault at 13:20Z independent of cache reads — e.g. CPU/worker saturation, a slow query, or an internal dependency step preceding the latency — would support H2.
- A deployment, feature-flag toggle or config change on any quote-path service (pricing-engine, market-data-service, api-gateway, order-service, exchange-gateway) at ~13:15-13:20Z that the change scans missed would reopen H3.
- Measured degradation of exchange-gateway p99, error_rate or upstream_latency_ms at the 13:20Z onset (or vendor-side telemetry showing an exchange outage) would revive H5.
- Evidence that other shared-redis-cache consumers degraded coincidentally for unrelated reasons, without any redis-cache dataset loss, would weaken the shared-cache causal claim and shift weight to a path-local explanation.
