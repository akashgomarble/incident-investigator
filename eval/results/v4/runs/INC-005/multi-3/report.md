# Incident investigation report: INC-005
*System: multi_agent | outcome: **root_cause_identified** | ended: critic_accepted*

## Summary
The root cause is the expiry of the mTLS client certificate CN=payments-client.orangeinvest.internal that payment-service presents to bank-partner-api: its notAfter is 2026-04-02T12:15:00Z, matching the failure onset to the second, after which every outbound transfer call is rejected at the TLS handshake ("tls: expired certificate (alert 45)"). The dashboard stayed green because payment-service's error_rate metric is documented as "% of requests (HTTP 5xx)" and the handshake rejection never surfaces as a handler-level 5xx — the failure instead appears as bank_api_error_rate going to 100% and payment_success_rate collapsing to ~2.5%. Deploy and config sweeps across all services for the onset window are empty, and the failure signature is confined to this one bank-partner-api caller (order-service, portfolio-service and settlement-service are clean on logs and metrics), which supports a per-caller credential expiry rather than a partner-side trust change (H4, 0.15). Confidence is high (0.85) but capped: no tool in the surface can read the keystore, so "the expired cert is the one actually loaded and presented" remains an assumption, and H4 cannot be falsified from our telemetry since bank-partner-api is external with no telemetry.

## Probable root cause
**Expired mTLS client certificate on payment-service → bank-partner-api edge** -- credential_expiry in `payment-service`, triggered by `null` (confidence 0.85, high)

### H1: Expired mTLS client certificate on payment-service → bank-partner-api edge
The client certificate CN=payments-client.orangeinvest.internal that payment-service presents to bank-partner-api reached notAfter=2026-04-02T12:15:00Z with no renewal automation. From that instant every outbound transfer call fails at the TLS handshake (alert 45, expired certificate), so transfers never settle. Because the failure occurs in the bank-client path and is a fast handshake rejection rather than an HTTP 5xx surfaced on the handler, payment-service's error_rate panel (documented as '% of requests (HTTP 5xx)') stays flat while payment_success_rate collapses — the 'dashboard green, customers failing' signature. The trigger is a credential state transition (expiry), not a pushed deploy or config change; every deploy/config sweep across ALL services is empty for the onset window. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: caller-side logs alone cannot prove the presented cert is ours-and-expired rather than a valid cert rejected by partner policy (H4); the fix covers both (renew/rotate AND partner escalation with the alert-45 trace).

### H4: Partner-side trust/policy change or rejection at bank-partner-api (out-of-band, invisible to our change feed)
The bank/partner side rejected payment-service's client certificate at the mTLS handshake — a partner-side trust-store/validation-policy change, or a rejection of a cert we believe valid. It predicts the IDENTICAL caller-side signature to H1 ('tls: expired certificate (alert 45)') because alert 45 is emitted by the partner's TLS stack, not ours, and it is consistent with the reporter's 'bank side' suspicion and the total absence of a change record on our side. Confidence is lowered from 0.30 because the discriminating per-caller evidence now points the other way: a partner trust-store/policy change that rejects payment-service's cert would be expected to sit on the partner's side of the trust boundary and not selectively break one caller while settlement-service, the other bank-partner-api caller, stays perfectly clean on logs and metrics. It remains unfalsifiable from our toolset (has_telemetry=false, no keystore read, no partner change feed), so it is not rejected — only reduced in rank. The two hypotheses imply different fixes (rotate our cert vs. escalate to the partner), so the recommended immediate action covers both.

3. Transfers fail at the handshake and never settle, collapsing payment_success_rate to ~2.5% while latency falls (fail-fast handshake rejection) [F5, F6]
